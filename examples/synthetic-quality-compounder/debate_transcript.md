# Debate transcript — ACME Industries

**Paper:** FER-SYN-2026-Q2-ACME
**Config:** 6 turns, 400 words max per turn, bull/bear/judge

---

### Turn 1 — Bull (defends the long)

The thesis hinges on three claims, each grounded in the appendix. First, ACME's ROIC has averaged 24.1% over five years (`row_roic_5yr`), placing it above the 21.4% top-decile threshold (`row_peer_roic`) — this is not a forecast, it is a track record. Second, the moat is verified via primary sources: nine distributors reported zero attrition (`row_channel_attrition`); customer concentration at 38% top-10 is below typical industrial-components warning thresholds (`row_top10_concentration`). Third, the EV opportunity is concrete: ACME is qualified at three of five top EV platforms (`row_ev_platforms_qualified`), and the industry TAM is $4.2B by 2028 (`row_ev_tam_2028`). The base case applies an 80% conversion to qualified volumes — a conservative factor against ACME's historical commercial conversion rate in industrial-automation work, which has been 88–92%. Take 80% × 13.5% mid-share × $4.2B TAM and you arrive at $454M of mature incremental revenue; at 20% gross margin and the company's operating leverage, this adds ~$0.95 to FY28 EPS over a base business worth ~$4.70. We model FY28 EPS at $5.65 and re-rate to 22× on the strength of the higher-growth mix. Position is sized 1.5%, entered below $84, with a kill switch on both EV revenue growth and gross margin contraction. The thesis is conservative because the kill switch fires mechanically before the bear's most likely scenario plays out.

### Turn 2 — Bear (attacks)

attack_type: `precedent`

The 80% conversion is exactly where I attack. Three comparable precision-components issuers ran the same playbook in EV ramps from 2018–2021: PRECISN (10-K FY21 p.38) realized 70% of underwritten conversion; HORTON (FY20 10-K p.41) realized 60%; ATLAS-PE (FY22 10-K p.44) realized 75%. Median is 68%, not 80%. The "88–92% conversion rate in industrial-automation work" the Bull cited is for *mature* qualified parts, not for *ramping* qualified parts where competitive re-qualification windows are open. The Helix ledger contains POS-2022-IND-014, structurally analogous, which closed at -8% versus an underwritten +30%. At 68% conversion, incremental revenue is $385M, FY28 EPS is approximately $5.35, IRR drops to ~8%. The base case is therefore the *Bull case*; the *actual* base case is below the desk's 10% IRR threshold. The kill switch on EV revenue growth (35% rolling 12-month) is structured to fire only if the situation collapses, not if it merely disappoints by the precedent-implied margin.

### Turn 3 — Bull (responds)

The Bear's three precedents are real, but they are not ACME's situation. PRECISN had one qualified platform; HORTON had two; ATLAS-PE had one. ACME has three of the top five, plus a tooling-library scale (`row_ma_history_fy18_fy25` shows 11,400 active part numbers) that none of the precedents matched. The conversion analog should be weighted by platform density — a denser qualified base is more competitively defensible because each platform's program managers have visibility into ACME on adjacent parts. I concede the *precedent set exists* and that the 80% baseline is on the high end. I do not concede that the median of the precedent set is the right anchor. The honest revision is a sensitivity table; I would model 75% as the base case (FY28 EPS ~$5.50, target $121, IRR ~10%) and present 68/75/80 as a bear/base/bull on conversion specifically. The thesis survives at $121 target; entry rule moves to $80 or below, position sized down to 1.2%. Not killed.

### Turn 4 — Bear (responds)

I take the concession on the 75% base case, which gives back $0.15 of FY28 EPS and ~$3 of target price — small but material. I now attack a different load-bearing node: the multiple re-rating from 18× to 22×. The Bull's argument for re-rating is *higher-growth mix*. The paper's growth-mix shift is real but modest: EV power-electronics moves from ~25% of revenue today to ~37% at FY28 in the revised base case. Comparable industrial-components issuers with similar EV-mix shifts (PRECISN post-2021, ATLAS-PE post-2022) traded at 16–19× through their ramp, not 22×. The 22× target is the *peak* multiple in the peer group, not the through-cycle median. If we anchor to 19× — the peer-group median for industrial issuers at this growth profile — FY28 target is $5.50 × 19× = $104.50, return at $84 entry is 24% over 3 years, IRR ~7.5%. The thesis is now *below* the desk's IRR threshold even with the Bull's revisions.

### Turn 5 — Bull (responds)

The 22× multiple is the bull-case multiple; the *base case* in my revised model is 20× — between the peer median 19× and the historical ACME multiple peaks of 22–24× during prior ramps. At 20× FY28 EPS of $5.50, target is $110, return at $84 entry is 31% over 3 years, IRR ~9.5%. Marginally below the 10% threshold; this is where the position should size down further, not where the thesis dies. The case for 20× over 19× rests on ACME's superior ROIC: peer median ROIC over the comparable periods was 17–19%; ACME at 24% has a documented 5pp ROIC advantage. The DCF math says each 1pp of ROIC delta supports ~0.6× of multiple expansion in stable-growth periods. Five pp = 3× of multiple expansion over the peer median; 19× + 3× = 22× would be the implied target, but I am marking it down to 20× to control for cycle position. Position sized at 1.0% (not 1.5%) and entry rule tightened to $78. Thesis still holds, but at a smaller size and tighter entry.

### Turn 6 — Bear (closing)

The Bull has now revised the position twice during the debate: from $5.65 / 22× / 1.5% / $84 entry, down to $5.50 / 20× / 1.0% / $78 entry. The revised thesis has an IRR of ~9.5% — below the desk's 10% threshold — and rests on a 20× multiple that is above the peer median. Two of the three load-bearing nodes have moved during a six-turn debate. I do not claim the thesis is wrong. I claim that the published paper, as drafted, oversells the conversion factor and the multiple, and that the *honest* position is closer to an opportunity at ~$70 entry than a thesis at $84. My closing recommendation to the Judge: paper does not ship in its current form. It ships as a rewritten paper with conversion-rate sensitivity baked in, the multiple framed against peer median, and the size and entry adjusted accordingly. That is not killing the position; that is forcing the desk to publish what it actually believes.
