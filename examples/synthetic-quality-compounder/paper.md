## One-page summary

ACME Industries is a precision industrial components manufacturer with top-decile ROIC and a multi-year capacity-buildout in EV power-electronics. The market values the current business at 18× forward earnings; we believe the EV opportunity adds ~25% to mid-cycle earnings power and warrants a re-rating to 22×.

**Entry:** below $84 (current $87.40).
**Exit:** $124 (FY28 EPS $5.65 × 22.0×) or kill switch.
**Kill switch:** quantitative — paper invalidates if (a) EV power-electronics revenue grows less than 35% in any rolling 12-month window through FY27, OR (b) gross margin contracts more than 200bp from FY25 levels at constant mix.
**Horizon:** 36 months.
**Expected return (base case):** ~42% over 3 years; IRR ~12%.

## Business overview

ACME manufactures high-precision components for industrial automation (~60% of revenue) and EV power-electronics (~25% and growing). Remaining 15% is aerospace specialty work, mostly mature. Customer base is concentrated in tier-1 industrial OEMs; top 10 customers represent 38% of revenue.

## Economic engine

ROIC has averaged 24% over the past five years (FY20–FY25), top decile in the industrial-components peer set. Drivers: (a) precision tolerances that command 18–22% gross margins versus 9–12% for commodity competitors; (b) capital efficiency from a vertically integrated tooling shop; (c) operating leverage on a fixed manufacturing footprint.

## Durability

The moat has two layers. First, ISO-9100 and customer-specific qualifications take 18–24 months to obtain; switching costs for OEMs are material once a part is qualified. Second, ACME's tooling library — 11,400 active part numbers as of FY25 — is a proprietary database competitors cannot replicate quickly.

We tested durability via primary-source channel checks across nine distributors (Apr 2026). No distributor reported customer attrition or active second-sourcing programs targeting ACME parts. Our base case further assumes no regulatory or technology disruption to the precision-machining moat through FY28 (`a_no_disruption`).

## Growth runway

EV power-electronics is the runway. Industry estimates put the addressable market for precision-machined power-module components at ~$4.2B by 2028 (CAGR 28% from $1.9B in 2025). ACME's qualified position with three top-five EV platform suppliers gives a credible path to 12–15% market share at maturity, implying $500–650M of incremental revenue at maturity at 19–21% gross margins (`a_ev_qualified_to_share`).

Capital intensity for the expansion: ~$240M cumulative through FY28, fully fundable from operating cash flow (`a_capex_self_funded`).

## Management and capital allocation

Founder-led; CEO has been at ACME 18 years. Capital allocation history (FY18–FY25): 62% reinvestment (capex + R&D), 23% acquisitions (3 tuck-ins at average 7× EBITDA), 15% buybacks. No special dividends. Acquisition track record: 2 of 3 deals achieved underwritten synergies within 24 months; the third underperformed in part due to a customer concentration issue not flagged in diligence. We assume continued capital-allocation discipline through FY28 (`a_capital_allocation_consistency`).

## Financial model

Base case (FY28E): revenue $1.84B (CAGR 11% from FY25 $1.34B), gross margin 38.5% (vs FY25 37.1%, with `a_margin_persist` underlying the trajectory at constant mix), operating margin 22.0% (vs FY25 19.8%), EPS $5.65. Bull case EPS $6.40; bear case $4.20.

The base case embeds the EV ramp at ~80% of qualified-platform volumes converting to ACME parts. Bull assumes 100%; bear assumes 50%.

## Valuation and entry

Current market: 18.0× FY26E EPS of $4.85 = $87.30. Our base FY28 EPS at 22× = $124.30, implying 42% return over 3 years and an IRR of 12%.

Entry below $84; exit $124 or kill switch. Position sized at 1.5% of model portfolio.

## Risks and counters

**R1.** EV ramp slips. *Counter:* the kill switch is quantitative on EV revenue growth; the position exits mechanically before the thesis broadly breaks.

**R2.** Industrial-automation cycle rolls over. *Counter:* historical peak-to-trough decline in this segment has been 18% over 14 months; the position's 22% bear-case EPS already incorporates a comparable cycle.

**R3.** Aerospace specialty business decline. *Counter:* this segment is 15% of revenue and ~9% of operating profit; full erosion reduces FY28 EPS by ~$0.45, still inside our base case.

## Reflexivity

If this view becomes consensus before the FY27 capacity ramp shows in results, the multiple expansion compresses our IRR by ~30%. Our entry rule incorporates this — we size down 20% for every 50bp of consensus FY28 EPS upgrade above the current $5.40 figure. We exit half the position if Street median reaches $5.50 (FY28E), regardless of price.
