### Turn 1 — Bull (defends the short)

Three facts make GLTZ a textbook unwind. (1) Inventory days expanded 88 → 142 over six quarters (`cite_inv_q1fy24`, `cite_inv_q1fy26`), a 61% increase that cannot be explained by seasonal mix at a brand that hasn't grown unit-volume in two years. (2) Same-store sales declined -3% in each of the last three reported quarters (`cite_sss_q3`, `cite_sss_q4`, `cite_sss_q1`); this is not noise. (3) The under-25 demographic — the core customer — shrank from 48% to 32% of revenue over three years (`cite_demo_fy23`, `cite_demo_fy26`), and category-wide data shows this is *GLTZ-specific*, not industry-wide. Stack these on a multiple of 28× FY26E EPS vs. peer median 18× (`cite_peer_mult`), and the unwind catalyst is the next two prints. Target $48 (16× $3.00). Position sized 1.0% with 95% borrow headroom (`cite_borrow`). Net of borrow, expected return ~38% over 18 months.

### Turn 2 — Bear (defends the long)

attack_type: `data_contradiction`

The Bull elides the off-price recognition story. The paper's body says "~$110M of off-price recognized at full wholesale" — but GLTZ's 10-K does not state the recognition policy. The agent's reconstruction depends on the assumption that off-price is recognized at full wholesale. Industry analogs (Coach FY18, Tapestry FY20) recognize off-price at *net realization*, not gross. If GLTZ does the same, the EPS misstatement is much smaller — closer to $0.06/sh than the implied $0.13/sh. The Bull's thesis on multiple compression may stand, but the "aggressive accounting" framing collapses.

### Turn 3 — Bull

The counter-construction agent already addressed this and the paper's appendix concedes the dependency. The Bull's central thesis is the multiple compression, not the off-price misstatement. Take the off-price away entirely. SSS is declining -3% across three quarters. Inventory days expanded 61% over six quarters. The under-25 cohort shrank 16pp in three years (GLTZ-specific). These three facts alone justify multiple compression from 28× to peer median 18× over 18 months. The target compresses from $48 to $54 if I credit the Bear's off-price framing — that's a -36% return on the stock, not -43%. Still well above the desk's threshold for a short.

### Turn 4 — Bear

### Turn 5 — Bull

42% recovery base rate is the headline; the median *time* to recovery in those 5 cases was 6 quarters, not 4. The paper's catalyst horizon is 18 months = 6 quarters. The thesis carries through the lag. The Bull's kill switch on SSS returning to ≥ +2% for two consecutive quarters is *exactly* the mechanical exit at the first sign of brand-led reacceleration. If the Bear's 42%-base-rate-with-6-quarter-lag scenario plays out, the short exits at the +2% print with modest loss — say $74 entry-revised position, -12% loss before borrow. Short-squeeze: borrow utilization is 5.1% of available; the position cannot be squeezed to a forced cover.

### Turn 6 — Bear

attack_type: `macro`

The Bull's thesis depends on consumer-discretionary spending continuing to decelerate. Consensus macro view: rates cut by Fed Sept FY26, consumer recovery accelerates Q1 FY27. If consumer spending re-accelerates, the under-25 cohort returns; GLTZ inventory clears; the multiple compression stalls. The kill switch on inventory days < 100 for two consecutive quarters fires *late* — by the time inventory clears, the multiple has likely already begun expanding. The Bull's risk is being right on fundamentals and wrong on timing.

### Turn 7 — Bull

The macro turn is fair, but two things break it. (1) GLTZ's deterioration began *before* macro slowdown (FY24 Q1 SSS was already +0.5% versus the category at +6%, then went negative in late FY24 while category remained positive). The shift is brand-specific, not macro-driven. (2) Multiple compression typically *leads* fundamentals by 6–9 months in this sub-sector — the kill switch on multiple > 22× for two consecutive quarters captures the timing risk. If the Bear's macro path plays out, the Bull exits at $76–80 mid-thesis, a -3% to -7% loss. The asymmetric risk profile cuts both ways and the kill switch is the asymmetry handler.
